In 2019, Kashechewan's chief, Ontario's Indigenous affairs minister and the federal Indigenous services minister signed a framework agreement confirming plans to relocate Kashechewan First Nation to higher ground, so the long-term plan is to move the community away from its flood-prone site.

A key step in the relocation is transferring provincial lands to Canada so they can be designated as reserve land, and an initial estimate put the cost of the relocation at more than 800 million dollars.

The school in Kashechewan was built on pilings to make a future move to the planned new location, called Site 5, easier.

In April the province's natural resources ministry upgraded its flood assessment from a flood watch to a flood warning. On April 13, 2021 the community confirmed that all dike flood gates were closed and its protection system was secure, and Canada funded 200 temporary shelters for precautionary evacuations onto the land in 2021.
